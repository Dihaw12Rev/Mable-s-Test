01 · How to use this guide

Read it once, then work from the steps

This guide replaces the earlier markdown documents. Chapter 05 is the procedure; everything else supports it. The appendices hold the full task library, so nobody has to open HubSpot Work to see what a hub includes.

Blank beats invented

Leave unknown values empty and create a follow-up. Never fill a number with a guess.

Evidence before Done

A task is Done when its definition of done is met and checked, not when it looks finished.

People own the customer

Agents draft, fill fields or flag. Every customer message and commitment is human.

Day 0 is payment

Start date is the day the deal reached Start Payment Received. Kickoff is Days 5–10.

One row per engagement

Every task, request and update links to its Customers row. A later onboarding gets a new row.

No silent scope

New work goes through a request, a price and an approval.

Status on 2 November 2026. The workspace structure and the data-rule workflows are live. Task creation, the handoff brief and the delivery agents are not yet running. Until they are, follow the manual fallback written into each step. Chapter 11 lists what's left before go-live.

03 · Roles and decision rights

Who does what

Phil leads through the sale and the handoff. After the handoff call the Architect is the strategy lead, including the what's next call. Owner role says who acts; Assignee is always a real internal person, even when the customer has the action.

OpsRevGravy operations. Owns the templates, Supered setup and the welcome emails.

05 · The procedure

Step by step, from sale to six months after

Each step names when it happens, who acts, what they do, and the condition that closes it. Task IDs refer to Appendix A.

01

Prepare the sales handoff

Before Day 0 · P0Phil
  • Write the research brief from the company website and attach it to the deal.
  • Build the FigJam canvas after the Fit call: strategic motivation, tactical outcomes, progress measures, fee range.
  • Write the From→To statement: one sentence from where they are to where they want to be.
  • Get the tech stack and team capacity survey back from the customer.
  • Put products, quote and hub tiers on the deal. Every hub sold is Professional or Enterprise.
  • Pick the progress measures for each hub sold (Appendix B), with baselines where known.
  • Confirm the POC, supporters and decision maker from contact labels or explicit conversations. Never infer authority from a billing label or a job title.
  • Get the agreement signed: AI terms, escalation path, how progress is measured, $250 requests with urgency classes.

Done when There is documented authority to start, scope is understood, and every missing item has a named owner. Missing evidence can carry into onboarding, but it stays visibly open.

02

Pick the Architect and add the customer

Day 0 · payment receivedPhilOps
  • Phil opens the Capacity report (active customers per Architect) and picks the Architect.
  • Ops adds one Customers row within 4 business hours: Customer, Company, Deal, Architect, Program length (60, 75 or 90), Start date, Products purchased, Hub tiers, From→To goal, FigJam and Supered links.
  • Start date is Day 0: the day the deal reached Start Payment Received. It is never the kickoff date.
  • Leave unknown values blank and create a follow-up task. Blank beats invented.
  • Ops ticks Create tasks.
  • Welcome email #1 goes out from Phil: the Supered Action Plan, the kickoff booking link and the first to-dos.

Done when The Customers row is complete, the Company and Deal are linked, and Create tasks is ticked.

03

Create the customer's tasks

Day 0HubSpot WorkOps
  • The Onboarding Task Generator copies the Onboarding Library once, plus one library per product purchased, into Customer Task.
  • It fills each task's Project Day 0 and Program length so the Due date formula calculates, and assigns the Architect's tasks.
  • It sets Target end date, adds the products to Tasks created for, and unticks Create tasks.
  • Ops checks the count: Core 58, plus Marketing 16, Sales 14, Service 11.
  • Fallback while the agent is paused: Ops copies the rows by hand and fills the same fields.

Done when Tasks created for lists every product purchased, and the task count matches.

04

Run the internal handoff

Days 1–3PhilArchitect
  • Read the Handoff Brief draft in the Update table. Until that agent is switched on, Phil and the Architect prepare the brief by hand.
  • Hold a 30-minute handoff call: what was bought, the outcome, baselines and targets, people and decision path, access needs, risks, the first customer commitment.
  • The Architect walks the P0 checklist and marks each item Done only with evidence. Anything missing goes back to Phil, who fixes it within 24 hours.
  • The Architect sets Won't do, with a reason, on anything the customer didn't buy (optional builds, Enterprise-only items for a Pro customer).
  • The Architect writes the progress measures with baseline, target, source and owner into the Customers row.
  • Every unresolved question becomes a linked task with an owner and a date.
  • From here on, the Architect is the strategy lead.

Done when P0 is closed or every gap is owned, the measures are recorded, and the brief is marked Reviewed.

05

Welcome, access and intake

Days 0–7 · P1–P2ArchitectOpsCustomer
  • Ops builds the Supered Action Plan from the template for the products bought.
  • Welcome email #2 goes out on Day 2: office hours, Friday updates, how to send requests and the urgency classes, escalation path, how we use AI.
  • The customer grants HubSpot access, fills the intake form, shares access to connected systems and old tools, and uploads brand files.
  • Customer-owned tasks sit in Waiting on client. The Architect marks them Done only once the access works or the form is in. Silence is not access.
  • Chase on Day 3. On Day 7, escalate to Phil and set Health to At risk.
  • The Architect runs the portal audit and records baseline numbers.
  • Credentials live only in the approved secure system, never in task notes.

Done when Access is granted, the intake is in, and baselines are recorded.

06

Kickoff

Days 5–10 · P3ArchitectCustomer
  • Draft the agenda from the intake and the audit.
  • In a 15-minute call: introduce the team; confirm the goal, scope and plan; walk through missing items; explain office hours, Friday updates, MBRs and requests.
  • Book MBR #1 (about Day 30), MBR #2 (about Day 60), the what's next call (about 30 days before the end) and the final results review. Scale these for 60- and 75-day programs.
  • Fill Next MBR on the Customers row. Confirm who needs which training modules.
  • Send the recap within 24 hours and update the Supered plan.

Done when The plan is confirmed, the meetings are on calendars, and the recap is sent.

07

Foundations

Days 7–30 · P4Architect
  • Work through the Core foundation tasks (account, data model, import, integrations, baseline dashboard) and each hub's foundation tasks.
  • A task is Done when every subtask is ticked or marked not applicable.
  • Tasks blocked on the customer's IT (DNS, for example) go to Waiting on client.
  • Foundation QA (4.11): test the main flow end to end for every hub sold.
  • Record a Supered video: what changed for the customer and why it matters.

Done when Foundation QA passes and the baseline dashboard is live.

08

Build and launch

Days 21–75 · P5–P6ArchitectCustomer
  • Build one progress measure at a time, in the order of the hub's library.
  • A build sits in In review while it waits for the customer's sign-off, and is Done only after sign-off and go-live.
  • Record a Supered video per delivery, and deliver the matching training module as each feature ships.
  • Migrations: rebuild, send in parallel once, then switch the old tool off with the customer's confirmation.
  • Day 45 and Day 75: adoption checks. Attendance alone is not adoption.

Done when Every progress measure sold is live, signed off and reporting.

09

Run the weekly rhythm

Day 7 to the end · P7ArchitectPhilHubSpot Work
  • Monday: the Architect plans the week from My tasks and This week.
  • Every day: review overdue tasks and separate internal delays from Waiting on client.
  • Friday: send the update in five parts: work done and the strategic issue it moves, numbers vs baseline, issues found outside scope, next week, the customer's to-dos. The Update row goes Draft → Reviewed → Sent.
  • Set Health weekly from evidence. On track means credible; At risk means a slip is likely but recoverable; Off track means a milestone is already jeopardized.
  • Phil runs a weekly delivery review with the Architects using the Health board, the Overdue view and the Capacity report.
  • MBRs compare sourced actuals with baseline and target.

Done when Recurring tasks stay In progress until close-out. Each Sent update is the record that the week happened.

10

Handle requests

Any timeArchitectCustomer
  • Customer asks: By email, Slack, in office hours or in Supered.
  • Logged the same day: The Architect adds a Requests row: Customer, Request, Type = Client request, Logged on = today.
  • Agent triage: Request Triage sets Urgency, Price and Due date. A Critical request alerts the Architect and Phil.
  • Customer confirms: The Architect checks the triage, then tells the customer the class, turnaround and price. Paid requests wait for their OK. Fixing something we built never does.
  • Work: New → In progress once they say yes → Done. Bigger than about 2 hours → Quoted → a proposal. An idea for later → Parked.
  • Billing: Suggested: Ops bills every Done request with a price at the end of the month.
  • What we find ourselves: Issues the Architect finds are logged as Issue found, ideas as Client idea. Both feed the Friday update and the what's next call.

Done when Done means the result is verified and communicated, with the customer's validation captured where needed.

11

What's next

About 35 to 25 days before the end · P8ArchitectPhil
  • The Architect prepares Option A and Option B from results, issues found and 'not now' ideas, and Phil reviews them.
  • The Architect runs the what's next call: results so far, long-term vision, the A/B choice, and the 6 months of free KPI monitoring.
  • The chosen option becomes a new deal in the Sales Pipeline.
  • New work is never added silently to the original scope. It goes through a linked request and approval.

Done when The customer has chosen a next step, and the proposal is sent.

12

Graduate

Last week of the programArchitectOps
  • Run the final results review: before vs after for every progress measure, what was built, what's still open.
  • Deliver the admin handbook in Supered.
  • Check the graduation gate (below). Mark every remaining task Done or Won't do, including the recurring ones.
  • Send the thank-you, the feedback survey and the testimonial ask. Ops archives the Supered plan.
  • Set up the monthly dashboard email for the monitoring period.

Done when The graduation gate is met. Health keeps its last value; it is not a completion status.

13

After the program

6 months after the end · P9Architect
  • Monthly: the KPI snapshot email with 3 short notes from the Architect.
  • Act on portal health alerts: deliverability drops, workflow errors, form submissions falling.
  • Check-ins at month 3 and month 6. At month 5, present the paid options.
  • A paid offer becomes a new deal. With no paid offer, set Current section to Completed at month 6.

Done when The customer is on a paid offer, or monitoring has ended cleanly.

Graduation gate

  • Contracted scope is delivered, or explicitly waived with a recorded reason.
  • Customer acceptance is captured where required.
  • Training is complete, and the customer knows how to get support.
  • Every progress measure has a final value from a real source and a continuing owner.
  • Critical blockers are resolved or formally handed to an owner.
  • Every open task and request is finished, transferred, or parked with the customer's agreement.
  • The customer has agreed what happens next and received a short closeout recap.

Only then set Current section to Completed. Keep the row as history; a later onboarding gets a new row.

06 · Requests

$250 per request, handled by urgency

The Request table is internal. Customers ask by email, Slack, in office hours or in Supered, and the Architect logs it the same day.

  • One request = one deliverable that takes up to about 2 hours (one email, one form, one workflow change, one report).
  • Bigger than that becomes a scoped quote, not a request.
  • Anything in the customer's plan (Task Template) is never a request.

10 · When things don't go to plan

Exceptions

SituationWhat to do
The customer is slow with access or the intakeTask goes to Waiting on client. Chase on Day 3. On Day 7, escalate to Phil using the escalation path and set Health to At risk.
A pre-sale item is missing at handoffThe Architect flags it on the P0 task. Phil fixes it within 24 hours.
The customer wants to pauseSet Health to Off track with a note. If the pause is longer than 2 weeks, move the Start date forward by the pause length. The Task Generator re-dates every task.
The Architect changesChange the Architect on the Customers row. The Task Generator moves the open Architect tasks to the new person.
The program length changesChange Program length (60, 75 or 90). Every due date recalculates.
A due date lands on a weekend or holidayDates are calendar days, so treat it as the next business day. In the example, Day 24 is Thanksgiving, Day 53 is Christmas and Day 60 is New Year's Day.
Something live breaksLog it as a Critical request. Fix it within 1 business day. It's free if it's in something we built.
The customer wants to buy more mid-programLog a request. It becomes Quoted, then a deal. This program's tasks don't change.
The customer buys another hub mid-programAdd it to Products purchased and tick Create tasks. The Task Generator adds only that hub's tasks, dated from that day. Core tasks aren't duplicated.
Agreement or approval is missingKeep the engagement out of active delivery until a start is authorized. Record the gap, an owner and the next action.
A draft contains a wrong or unsupported claimDon't share it. Fix the source record, revise the draft, and have the Architect confirm the evidence.
The program ends with open itemsAgree item by item: finish, hand to support, or park. Don't set Completed until each has an owner.
